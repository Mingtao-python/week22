Physics: Classical Mechanics

Chapter 1: Kinematics

1.1 Basic Concepts
Kinematics is the study of motion without considering the forces causing it.

Key definitions:
- Displacement (s or Δx): Change in position (vector quantity)
- Velocity (v): Rate of change of displacement (vector quantity)
  Average velocity = Δx / Δt
  Instantaneous velocity = ds/dt (derivative of position)
- Acceleration (a): Rate of change of velocity
  Average acceleration = Δv / Δt
  Instantaneous acceleration = dv/dt (derivative of velocity)
- Speed: Magnitude of velocity (scalar quantity)

1.2 Uniformly Accelerated Motion
When acceleration is constant, we can use kinematic equations:
v = v₀ + at
s = v₀t + ½at²
v² = v₀² + 2as
s = ½(v₀ + v)t

Where:
- s = displacement
- v = final velocity
- v₀ = initial velocity
- a = acceleration
- t = time

Example: A car starts from rest (v₀ = 0) with acceleration a = 2 m/s² for 5 seconds.
v = 0 + 2(5) = 10 m/s
s = 0 + ½(2)(5²) = 25 m

1.3 Projectile Motion
Projectile motion occurs when an object is launched with an initial velocity at an angle.

Horizontal component: vₓ = v₀cos(θ) (constant)
Vertical component: vᵧ = v₀sin(θ) - gt (changes due to gravity)

Horizontal displacement: x = v₀cos(θ)t
Vertical displacement: y = v₀sin(θ)t - ½gt²

Maximum height: h = (v₀sin(θ))² / (2g)
Range: R = (v₀²sin(2θ)) / g

Chapter 2: Dynamics

2.1 Newton's Laws of Motion

First Law: An object at rest remains at rest, and an object in motion remains in motion unless acted upon by a net external force. This describes inertia.

Second Law: The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.
F = ma
Where F is the net force, m is mass, and a is acceleration.

Third Law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
If object A exerts a force on object B, then object B exerts an equal and opposite force on object A.

2.2 Types of Forces

Weight: The force of gravity acting on an object
W = mg (where g ≈ 9.8 m/s² on Earth)

Normal Force: The perpendicular contact force exerted by a surface

Friction: The force opposing motion
- Static friction: fs ≤ μs · N (prevents motion)
- Kinetic friction: fk = μk · N (opposes motion)
Where μ is the coefficient of friction and N is the normal force

Tension: The pulling force transmitted through a rope or cable

2.3 Applications of Newton's Laws

Inclined Plane: An object on a plane inclined at angle θ
- Component parallel to plane: mg sin(θ)
- Component perpendicular to plane: mg cos(θ)

Connected Objects: Use free-body diagrams to analyze forces
Example: Two masses connected by a rope over a pulley
For mass m₁ on table and m₂ hanging:
Net force = m₂g - friction force
Total mass = m₁ + m₂
Acceleration = (m₂g - μm₁g) / (m₁ + m₂)

Chapter 3: Work and Energy

3.1 Work
Work is done when a force causes a displacement in the direction of the force.
W = F · d · cos(θ)
Where F is force, d is displacement, and θ is the angle between them.

If force is in the direction of motion: W = Fd
If force is perpendicular to motion: W = 0

3.2 Energy

Kinetic Energy: Energy of motion
KE = ½mv²

Potential Energy: Stored energy due to position
Gravitational PE = mgh (where h is height above reference point)
Elastic PE = ½kx² (for springs, where k is spring constant, x is compression/extension)

3.3 Conservation of Energy
In an isolated system with no friction:
Total mechanical energy = KE + PE = constant

E₁ = E₂
KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂
½mv₁² + mgh₁ = ½mv₂² + mgh₂

Example: A ball dropped from height h
At top: E = mgh (all potential)
At bottom: E = ½mv² (all kinetic)
v = √(2gh)

3.4 Power
Power is the rate of doing work.
P = W/t (average power)
P = F · v (instantaneous power)
Unit: Watt (W) = Joule per second (J/s)

Chapter 4: Momentum and Collisions

4.1 Momentum
Momentum is the product of mass and velocity.
p = mv (vector quantity)

Impulse: Change in momentum
Impulse = F · Δt = Δp = m · Δv

4.2 Conservation of Momentum
In an isolated system (no external forces):
Total momentum before = Total momentum after
p₁ᵢ + p₂ᵢ = p₁f + p₂f

4.3 Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

Elastic collision: Kinetic energy is conserved
- Velocities exchange in a head-on collision between equal masses
- v₁f = ((m₁ - m₂)v₁ᵢ + 2m₂v₂ᵢ) / (m₁ + m₂)
- v₂f = ((m₂ - m₁)v₂ᵢ + 2m₁v₁ᵢ) / (m₁ + m₂)

Inelastic collision: Kinetic energy is not conserved (converted to heat, sound, deformation)
- Objects may stick together after collision
- Using momentum conservation: (m₁ + m₂)vf = m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ

Chapter 5: Circular Motion and Gravitation

5.1 Uniform Circular Motion
For an object moving in a circle at constant speed:
- Angular velocity: ω = θ/t = 2π/T (radians per second)
- Linear velocity: v = ωr
- Centripetal acceleration: ac = v²/r = ω²r
- Centripetal force: Fc = mac = mv²/r = mω²r

5.2 Newton's Law of Gravitation
F = G(m₁m₂/r²)
Where G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (gravitational constant)

Gravitational field strength: g = F/m = GM/r²

5.3 Orbital Motion
For circular orbits:
Fc = Fg
mv²/r = GMm/r²
v = √(GM/r)

Orbital period: T = 2πr/v = 2π√(r³/GM)

Kepler's Third Law: T² ∝ r³
For planets in our solar system: T² = (4π²/GM)r³
