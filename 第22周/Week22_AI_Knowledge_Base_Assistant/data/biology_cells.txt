Biology: Cell Structure and Function

Chapter 1: Introduction to Cells

1.1 Cell Theory
The cell is the basic unit of life. Key principles:
1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells
2. The cell is the smallest unit of life
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells

Cells were first observed by Robert Hooke in 1665 using an early microscope.

1.2 Cell Types
Prokaryotic cells: No nucleus or membrane-bound organelles
- Found in bacteria and archaea
- Simpler and smaller (typically 1-10 μm)
- No histones for DNA organization (usually)
- Single, circular DNA chromosome

Eukaryotic cells: Nucleus and membrane-bound organelles
- Found in animals, plants, fungi, and protists
- Larger (typically 10-100 μm)
- DNA organized by histones into chromosomes
- Multiple linear DNA chromosomes in nucleus

Chapter 2: Prokaryotic Cell Structure

2.1 Cell Membrane (Plasma Membrane)
Selectively permeable barrier surrounding the cell
- Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins
- Controls what enters and exits the cell

2.2 Cytoplasm
Gel-like substance filling the cell interior
- Contains ribosomes, plasmids, and other molecules

2.3 Nucleoid Region
Area where DNA is located (not enclosed by a membrane)
- Usually contains one circular DNA chromosome
- May contain plasmids (small, circular DNA molecules)

2.4 Cell Wall
Rigid outer layer (in bacteria)
- Provides structural support and protection
- Composed of peptidoglycan (unique to bacteria)

2.5 Ribosomes
Structures that synthesize proteins
- 70S ribosomes (smaller than eukaryotic)
- Located free in cytoplasm

2.6 Flagella and Pili
Flagella: Tail-like structures for movement
Pili: Hair-like projections for attachment and conjugation

Chapter 3: Eukaryotic Cell Structure

3.1 Nucleus
Double-membrane organelle containing most of the cell's DNA
- Nuclear envelope (nuclear membrane) controls entry/exit
- Nucleolus: Site of ribosomal RNA synthesis
- Chromatin: DNA and histone proteins
- During cell division, chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes

3.2 Ribosomes
80S ribosomes (larger than prokaryotic)
- Some free in cytoplasm (rough endoplasmic reticulum)
- Synthesize proteins

3.3 Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)
Network of membrane-bound channels

Rough ER:
- Studded with ribosomes
- Synthesizes proteins for secretion and membrane proteins
- Connected to the nuclear envelope

Smooth ER:
- No ribosomes
- Synthesizes lipids
- Involved in drug and detoxification metabolism
- Calcium storage in muscle cells

3.4 Golgi Apparatus
Stacked membrane sacs near the nucleus
- Receives proteins and lipids from ER
- Modifies, packages, and ships them in vesicles
- Functions: packaging and transport

3.5 Mitochondria
"Powerhouse of the cell"
- Double membrane organelle
- Site of aerobic cellular respiration
- Produces ATP (adenosine triphosphate) for energy
- Contains own DNA and ribosomes (prokaryotic origin)
- Outer membrane: Smooth
- Inner membrane: Highly folded into cristae
- Matrix: Inner space containing enzymes

3.6 Lysosomes
Membrane-bound sacs containing digestive enzymes
- Found mainly in animal cells
- Break down cellular waste and debris
- Autophagy: Process of lysosomes breaking down damaged organelles

3.7 Chloroplasts (Plant Cells)
Site of photosynthesis
- Double membrane organelle
- Contains thylakoids (stack into grana)
- Stroma: Gel-like interior with Calvin cycle enzymes
- Contains own DNA and ribosomes (prokaryotic origin)
- Green color from chlorophyll pigments

3.8 Vacuoles
Storage compartments

Animal cells: Small vacuoles, various functions
Plant cells: Large central vacuole
- Maintains turgor pressure (keeps plant firm)
- Stores water, nutrients, and waste
- Can occupy up to 90% of cell volume

3.9 Cytoskeleton
Network of protein filaments throughout cytoplasm

Components:
Microfilaments: Thin actin filaments
- Cell movement, muscle contraction, cytokinesis

Intermediate filaments: Strong structural elements
- Provide mechanical strength
- Include keratin in skin cells

Microtubules: Hollow tubulin cylinders
- Maintain cell shape
- Movement of organelles
- Component of centrioles and flagella
- Involved in mitosis and meiosis

3.10 Centrioles
Paired barrel-shaped structures (in animal cells)
- Located near the nucleus
- Composed of nine triplets of microtubules
- Function in cell division and formation of cilia/flagella

3.11 Cell Wall (Plant Cells)
Rigid outer layer surrounding the cell membrane
- Composed of cellulose
- Provides structural support
- Prevents excessive water uptake

3.12 Cilia and Flagella (Eukaryotic)
Cilia: Numerous, short hair-like structures
- Move substances along cell surface
- Found in respiratory tract, reproductive system

Flagella: Single or few, long tail-like structures
- Propel the cell through fluid
- Found in sperm cells and some protists

Chapter 4: Cell Membrane Structure and Function

4.1 Fluid Mosaic Model
Describes the cell membrane as:
- Fluid: Lipids and proteins move within the membrane
- Mosaic: Composed of different types of molecules

4.2 Phospholipid Bilayer
Basic structure of all cell membranes
- Hydrophilic heads face outward (toward water)
- Hydrophobic tails face inward (away from water)
- Selectively permeable to many substances

4.3 Membrane Proteins
Integral proteins: Span the entire membrane
- Often function as channels, carriers, or receptors

Peripheral proteins: Attached to membrane surface
- Provide structural support
- Involved in signaling

Functions:
- Transport
- Cell recognition
- Receptor sites
- Enzyme activity
- Cell-cell communication

4.4 Cholesterol and Carbohydrates
Cholesterol: Decreases membrane fluidity and increases stability
Carbohydrates: Form glycoproteins and glycolipids
- Cell recognition markers
- Immune response
- Blood type determination

Chapter 5: Transport Across Membranes

5.1 Passive Transport
No energy (ATP) required

Diffusion: Movement from high to low concentration
- Oxygen diffusion into cells
- Carbon dioxide diffusion out of cells

Osmosis: Diffusion of water across a semipermeable membrane
- Hypertonic solution: Water leaves cell (plasmolysis)
- Hypotonic solution: Water enters cell (turgid)
- Isotonic solution: Equal water movement (equilibrium)

Facilitated diffusion: Protein channels aid diffusion
- No ATP needed
- Moves glucose, ions
- Follows concentration gradient

5.2 Active Transport
Requires ATP energy

Moves substances against concentration gradient (low to high)
Examples:
- Sodium-potassium pump (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase)
- Maintains cell volume and potential difference
- Exchanges 3 Na⁺ out for 2 K⁺ in

5.3 Bulk Transport

Endocytosis: Cell takes in large materials
- Phagocytosis: Cell "eating" large particles or cells
- Pinocytosis: Cell "drinking" fluids and small molecules
- Receptor-mediated: Specific uptake via receptor proteins

Exocytosis: Cell releases large materials
- Vesicles fuse with cell membrane
- Releases hormones, neurotransmitters, proteins
